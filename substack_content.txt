Washington was my No. 7 defense and finished fifth with 14 points. New England was ranked ninth and finished ninth, Detroit was 10th and finished 10th, and Chicago was 12th and finished eighth.

Strong floor with one of the better sack ceilings of the week.
The Giants are my favorite streamer heading into Week 9, and the pressure matchup is a big reason why. Over their last four games, New York has generated pressure on 43.5% of opposing dropbacks, while Philadelphia has allowed pressure at a massive 46.8% rate. That is one of the strongest pressure matchups on the slate.
The Eagles have also struggled badly through the air recently, producing -0.193 EPA per pass over their last four games. Meanwhile, the Giants have been excellent against the pass, allowing -0.249 EPA per opposing pass play during that stretch. That combination gives New York a real advantage when Philadelphia has to throw.
The Sack Opportunity score comes in at 3.38, one of the highest projections of Week 9. Philadelphia has also turned 17.5% of its pressures into sacks, so this isn’t just a matchup where we expect the Giants to create pressure. There is a good chance those pressures turn into actual fantasy production.
The Vegas setup is also one of my favorites. New York is a 3.5-point home favorite, the game total is only 40, and Philadelphia is implied for just 18.3 points.
The one weakness is the run matchup. Philadelphia has struggled on the ground at -0.203 EPA per rush, but New York has allowed +0.026 EPA per rush. Still, everything else lines up extremely well.
Pressure, sacks, a struggling passing offense and one of the lowest opponent implied totals on the slate. This is exactly what I’m looking for from a streamer.

Higher risk, but plenty of upside.
Miami has become one of the better offenses to target for D/ST production. Over the last four games, the Dolphins have allowed pressure on 47.9% of their dropbacks, one of the highest rates in football. Detroit hasn’t been an elite pressure defense at 37.6%, but Miami has given opposing defenses plenty of opportunities to get after the quarterback.
Malik Willis has also taken sacks on 20% of his pressures, which helps push Detroit’s Sack Opportunity score to 2.82. That gives the Lions one of the stronger projected sack environments among the defenses available on waivers.
Miami has also struggled through the air, producing -0.131 EPA per pass over its last four games. The concern is Detroit’s pass defense, which has allowed +0.147 EPA per opposing pass play. That is easily the biggest weakness in this matchup and why I don’t view Detroit as quite as safe as the Giants.
The run matchup looks much better. Miami is producing +0.059 EPA per rush, but Detroit has been very strong against the run, allowing -0.165 EPA per rush over the last four games. If the Lions can slow down Miami on the ground, it could create more obvious passing situations and allow the pass rush to attack.
Vegas also gives us the game script we want. Detroit is a seven-point home favorite, which creates a good chance Miami is forced to throw more in the second half.
There is definitely some risk because of Detroit’s pass defense, but the combination of Miami’s pressure problems, Willis’ sack rate and a favorable expected game script gives the Lions a pretty strong ceiling.

Chicago doesn’t have the same elite pressure matchup as the Giants or Lions. The Bears are generating pressure at a respectable 39.9% rate, but Tampa Bay has only allowed pressure on 34.3% of dropbacks over its last four games.
What makes this matchup interesting is what happens when Baker Mayfield does get pressured. He has taken a sack on 26.4% of his pressures, one of the highest rates among the quarterbacks we’re tracking. That helps Chicago still reach a 2.42 Sack Opportunity score despite Tampa doing a solid job limiting pressure overall.
The Buccaneers have also been one of the least efficient passing offenses lately, producing -0.223 EPA per pass over their last four games. Chicago’s pass defense has been closer to average at +0.022 EPA allowed, but Tampa’s recent offensive struggles make the matchup much more appealing.
Chicago has also been excellent against the run, allowing -0.201 EPA per rush. Tampa Bay hasn’t done much on the ground either at -0.015 EPA per rush, so there isn’t an obvious area of the offense that scares me here.
The game script is another positive. Chicago is a six-point home favorite, while Tampa Bay is implied for only 20.3 points. If the Bears can get ahead and force Tampa into more passing situations, Baker’s tendency to take sacks when pressured could become very important.
This isn’t the best pure pressure matchup of the three, but I like the combination of recent offensive struggles, quarterback sack tendency, strong run defense and favorable Vegas environment.
