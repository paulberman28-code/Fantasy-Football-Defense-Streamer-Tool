Scoring Rules & Analytical Methodology

Comprehensive documentation of custom scoring calculations, Next Gen Stats definitions, and weather physics.

1. The WAFFLe's Customized D/ST Scoring System

Our projections and rankings calculate defensive fantasy points strictly tailored to the WAFFLe's customized rules:

Turnovers & Big Plays:
  • Sack Taken: 1 pt
  • Interception Thrown: 1 pt
  • Fumble Lost: 1 pt
  • Safety Allowed: 2 pts
  • Blocked Kick Allowed: 1 pt
  • Defensive Touchdown Allowed: 6 pts
Points Allowed Brackets (Dictated by Offense Score):
  • 0 Points Scored: 10 pts to Defense
  • 1–6 Points Scored: 7 pts to Defense
  • 7–13 Points Scored: 3 pts to Defense
  • 14+ Points Scored: 0 pts to Defense

2. Next Gen Stats & Advanced Metrics

Borrowing the analytical framework from leading NFL quant research:

  • Defensive Quality Index (DQI): Composite quality score (50–100 scale, average = 75.0) synthesizing defensive pressure generation (25%), defensive pass EPA allowed (25%), 3rd down stop rate (20%), takeaway drive rate (15%), and red zone touchdown prevention (15%). Identifies inherently elite defenses (e.g. Minnesota 95.4) regardless of matchup.
  • Defensive Pressure %: Share of opponent dropbacks where the defense creates pressure (hurry, knockdown, or sack). Defenses generating >25% pressure disrupt quarterback timing and force erratic throws.
  • Defensive Pass EPA Allowed: Expected Points Added allowed per pass play. Negative values represent lockdown pass coverage (e.g. Minnesota -0.158), while positive values indicate porous secondaries.
  • 3rd Down Stop %: Percentage of 3rd down attempts where the defense prevents a first down. Teams above 70% terminate opponent drives early and rack up punt opportunities.
  • Takeaway Drive %: Percentage of defensive drives ending in an interception or fumble recovery.
  • Red Zone TD Allowed %: Opponent touchdown conversion rate inside the 20-yard line. Elite red-zone defenses bend without breaking, preserving low-scoring fantasy brackets.
  • Key Defensive Injuries: Official injury report statuses (Out, DNP, Questionable, IR) filtering out veteran rest days to isolate impactful absences along the defensive line, edge rush, and secondary.
  • Avg Time to Throw (NGS TTT): Time from snap to release. QBs holding the ball >3.00s (e.g., Caleb Williams 3.24s, Drake Maye 3.14s) invite heavy pocket collapse and strip-sacks.
  • Tight-Window Throw % (NGS Agg%): Percentage of pass attempts where a defender is within 1 yard. High tight-window throwing leads to tipped passes and interceptions.
  • Sacks per Pressure % (S/P%): Measures QB sack avoidance under pressure. QBs like Bryce Young (34.8%), Daniel Jones (32.6%), and Baker Mayfield (36.8%) convert pressures to sacks at elite rates for opposing fantasy defenses.
  • Opponent Passing EPA (L4): Expected Points Added per pass play conceded by the opposing offense over the last 4 games. An offense in negative EPA territory (e.g. Chargers -0.365, Colts -0.215) is a prime streaming target.

3. Environmental Physics: Wind vs. Interceptions

While rain primarily causes slick fumbles, wind gusts of 20–25+ mph are the single most volatile environmental driver of interceptions in professional football:

Aerodynamic Instability

On throws beyond 10 air yards, crosswinds and sudden gusts buffet the ball, causing passes to drift, flutter, and sail high over receivers into waiting safeties.

Holding the Ball into Wind

When a QB holds the ball for an NFL-high 3.24s (Caleb Williams) in 25 mph wind gusts at Soldier Field, edge rushers force off-platform throws with diminished ball velocity that float directly into coverage.

Tipped-Ball Deflections

Receivers fighting wind gusts suffer higher drop and double-clutch rates. Balls bobbled off hands and shoulder pads pop directly into the air for defensive backs.
