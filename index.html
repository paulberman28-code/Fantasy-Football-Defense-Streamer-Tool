Scoring Rules and Glossary

Comprehensive reference guide and glossary covering custom fantasy scoring rules, projection formulas, advanced Next Gen Stats, defensive metrics, and table definitions.

1. The WAFFLe's Customized D/ST Scoring System

Our projections and rankings calculate defensive fantasy points strictly tailored to the WAFFLe's customized scoring format:

Turnovers, Big Plays & Special Teams:
  • Quarterback Sack: 1 pt
  • Interception Caught: 1 pt
  • Fumble Recovered: 1 pt
  • Safety Allowed: 2 pts
  • Blocked Kick (FG / Punt / XP): 1 pt
  • Defensive & ST Touchdown: 6 pts (Pick-six, fumble scoop-and-score, punt/kickoff return)
Points Allowed Brackets (Opponent Offensive Score):
  • 0 Points Scored: 10 pts to Defense
  • 1–6 Points Scored: 7 pts to Defense
  • 7–13 Points Scored: 3 pts to Defense
  • 14+ Points Scored: 0 pts to Defense

Vegas Calibration (17.0 Implied Cutoff): Because any opponent scoring 14+ points earns 0 fantasy points, our projection engine enforces a strict 17.0 Vegas implied point cutoff (5 points above the 7–13 pt bracket ceiling). Any offense implied for >17.0 points projects flat 0.0 bracket fantasy points, ensuring high-risk defenses derive 100% of their projection from genuine sack and takeaway potential.

2. Fantasy Projections & Streamer Verdicts Glossary

Definitions of all core projection and ranking columns featured on the Week 9 and Look-Ahead boards:

  • Projected Fantasy Points (Proj Pts): Mathematically weighted fantasy projection summing expected sacks (1.0), interceptions (1.0), fumbles recovered (1.0), defensive/ST touchdowns (6.0), safeties (2.0), and expected points-allowed bracket points.
  • Scoring Floor: Conservative downside baseline calculation: (0.55 × Proj Sacks) + (0.35 × Proj INTs) + (0.40 × Proj Bracket Pts). Measures bankable production in worst-case negative game scripts.
  • Projected Sacks, INTs & Fumbles: Individual component expectations derived from opposing offensive dropback rates, QB sack avoidance, tight-window throwing, and fumble frequencies.
  • Vegas Implied Opponent Score (Vegas Implied): Expected points scored by the opposing offense derived from consensus sportsbook totals and spreads: (Over-Under / 2) - (Spread / 2).
  • Projected Bracket Points (Proj Bracket Points): Expected fantasy points earned from holding an opponent to 0, 1–6, or 7–13 points, strictly zeroed out when the opponent's Vegas implied total exceeds 17.0 points.
  • Streamer Verdicts: Simplified four-tier decision framework:
    • Priority: Elite must-start units (≥7.0 proj pts) with high floors and dominant pressure/takeaway upside.
    • Speculative: High-upside streaming options (≥5.0 proj pts) targeting backup QBs, offensive line mismatches, or weather opportunities.
    • Neutral: Mid-tier floor plays (≥4.5 proj pts) or elite defenses facing formidable quarterbacks (e.g. Vikings vs. Josh Allen).
    • Avoid: High-risk matchups facing explosive offenses (implied 24+ points) with low sack or turnover projections.

⏱️ Analytical Time Horizons: Last 4 Games (L4) vs. Full Season (Szn)

Our model intentionally uses specialized statistical time horizons across different metric categories rather than forcing an artificial uniform window. Football analytics research demonstrates that offensive vulnerability, defensive talent, and turnover events stabilize at fundamentally different sample sizes:

1. Opposing Offensive Vulnerability — Last 4 Games (L4):

Why L4? Offenses and offensive lines fluctuate rapidly throughout an NFL season due to injuries, line rotations, scheme adjustments, and quarterback benchings (e.g. Brissett → Minshew, Watson → Sanders). Furthermore, research proves Sacks per Pressure (S/P%) is largely a quarterback-owned trait that stabilizes over 100–150 dropbacks (~3 to 4 games). Evaluating S/P% and Pass EPA over the last 4 games captures the true unit taking the field this Sunday rather than outdated September data.

2. Defensive Unit Quality — Full Season Baseline (Weeks 1–8):

Why Full Season? Defense is inherently reactive and opponent-dependent. Over any 4-game sample (~230 snaps), a defense's numbers can be heavily distorted by facing backup QBs or an offensive juggernaut. A full 8-game season baseline (~500 snaps) smooths out schedule variance and isolates genuine defensive talent (pressure generation, 3rd down stops, red zone defense).

3. Ball Security & Next Gen Stats — Full Season Baseline:

Why Full Season? Fumbles are low-frequency, high-variance events. A 4-game sample (~140 dropbacks) is pure noise where one fluke fumble swings a rate by 100–200%. Full-season dropbacks (250+) are necessary to identify true chronic fumblers (e.g. Baker Mayfield 4.8%, Caleb Williams 6 fumbles) from disciplined ball handlers.

3. Defensive Quality & Health Glossary (Full Season Baseline: Weeks 1–8)

Advanced metrics measuring the defense's inherent skill, efficiency, and roster health (evaluated over all games played in Weeks 1–8 to smooth opponent schedule variance):

  • Defensive Quality Index (DQI - Szn): Composite quality score (50–100 scale, league average = 75.0) synthesizing full-season defensive pressure generation (25%), pass EPA allowed (25%), 3rd down stop rate (20%), takeaway drive rate (15%), and red zone touchdown prevention (15%). Isolates true defensive prowess regardless of matchup.
  • Defensive Pressure % (Szn): Full-season share of opponent dropbacks where the defense creates pressure (hurry, knockdown, or sack). Units generating >25% pressure disrupt quarterback timing and force erratic throws.
  • Defensive Pass EPA Allowed (Szn): Full-season Expected Points Added allowed per pass play. Negative values represent lockdown pass coverage (e.g. Vikings -0.207), while positive values indicate porous secondaries.
  • 3rd Down Stop Rate % (Szn): Full-season percentage of opponent 3rd down attempts prevented from converting. Elite defenses (>70%, e.g. Bears 78.2%) terminate opponent drives early and pin offenses deep.
  • Takeaway Drive % (Szn): Full-season percentage of opponent offensive drives ending in an interception or fumble recovery. Primary engine of high-ceiling defensive fantasy performances.
  • Punt Drive % (Szn): Full-season percentage of opponent offensive possessions terminated in a punt. Measures defensive drive disruption and field position control.
  • Red Zone TD Allowed % (Szn): Full-season opponent touchdown conversion rate inside the 20-yard line. Elite red-zone defenses bend without breaking, holding offenses to field goals and preserving low-scoring brackets.
  • Def Health / Key Defensive Injuries: Current Week 9 official practice participation and injury report statuses (Out, DNP, Questionable, IR), filtering out routine veteran rest to isolate impactful absences along the defensive line, edge rush, and secondary.

4. Opposing Quarterback & Next Gen Stats Glossary (L4 Form + Season Baseline)

Metrics evaluating the opposing quarterback's tendencies, decision-making, and vulnerability (blending rolling Last 4 Games for form with full season for sample stability):

  • Sacks per Pressure % (S/P% - Last 4 Games): Percentage of pocket pressures converted into sacks over the opposing offense's last 4 games. Measures QB sack avoidance: passers like Bryce Young (34.8%), Daniel Jones (32.9%), and Baker Mayfield (36.8%) manufacture sacks for fantasy defenses.
  • Opponent Passing EPA (Off Pass EPA - Last 4 Games): Expected Points Added per pass play conceded by the opposing offense over their last 4 games. Captures current passing form and offensive line decline (e.g. Chargers -0.365 is a prime streaming target).
  • Opponent Points Allowed to D/ST (L2, L4, Total Szn): Historical fantasy points yielded by that offense to opposing defenses across recent games (L2, L4) and full season (Weeks 1–8).
  • QB Fumble Rate % & Fumbles (Season Baseline): Percentage of offensive dropbacks and touches where the quarterback commits a fumble all season. Full-season tracking separates chronic fumblers (e.g. Baker Mayfield 4.8%, Joe Fagnano 4.2%) from noise.
  • Avg Time to Throw (NGS TTT - Season Baseline): Full-season Next Gen Stats release time in seconds. QBs holding the ball >3.00s (e.g. Caleb Williams 3.24s, Malik Willis 3.05s) invite pocket collapse and blindside sacks.
  • Tight-Window Throw % (NGS Agg% - Season Baseline): Full-season Next Gen Stats aggressiveness rate (% of passes into <1 yard separation). Leads to tipped balls and interceptions.
  • Opposing QB & Status: Starting quarterback identified along with Starter or Backup designation and context (injury, benching).

6. Environmental Physics & Weather Modeling Glossary

While rain primarily causes slick fumbles, sustained winds and peak gusts of 20–25+ mph are the single most volatile environmental driver of interceptions in professional football:

Aerodynamic Instability

On throws beyond 10 air yards, crosswinds and sudden gusts buffet the football in flight, causing passes to drift, flutter, and sail high over receivers into waiting safeties.

Holding the Ball into Wind

When a QB holds the ball for >3.00s in gusty winds, edge rushers force off-platform throws with diminished ball velocity that float directly into coverage.

Tipped-Ball Deflections

Receivers fighting wind gusts suffer higher drop and double-clutch rates. Balls bobbled off hands and shoulder pads pop directly into the air for defensive backs.

Slick Turf & Ball Security

Precipitation (≥0.05" rain or snow) creates slick leather footballs and slippery turf, significantly elevating strip-sack and loose-ball fumble recovery rates.

Indoor Dome Advantage

Climate-controlled stadiums eliminate weather variance, providing clean track conditions for high-pressure fronts to operate at maximum speed on turf.
